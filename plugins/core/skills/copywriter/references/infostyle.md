# Infostyle: writing in the reader's world

Principles drawn from the Russian editorial school of инфостиль (Maxim Ilyakhov and Lyudmila
Sarycheva's *Пиши, сокращай*; Ilyakhov's *Ясно, понятно*), adapted to this skill. Read this for
simplification, explanations, marketing and product copy, any text a reader has to understand
quickly, and any Russian-language work.

One adjustment up front. Infostyle as popularly practised pushes toward the telegram: every
qualifier gone, every sentence a fact. `evidence.md` shows that text sanded that far reads as
machine-written. So this file takes the principles and leaves the compression reflex behind. The
goal is a text a person would read to the end because it respects their time, not one that has
survived a stop-word filter.

## The principles

Infostyle reduces to a handful of commitments. Everything else is technique.

1. **The text exists for the reader, not the author.** Every decision is made from their seat: what
   they know, what they're afraid of, what they'll do next.
2. **Facts, not evaluations.** The reader forms the opinion. The writer supplies what the opinion
   would be based on.
3. **Truth, including the inconvenient parts.** A text that hides the catch loses the reader the
   moment they find it elsewhere.
4. **Attention is borrowed.** The reader gives it grudgingly and takes it back at the first sign of
   waste. Every sentence either pays them for it or spends it.
5. **Care over politeness.** "Please" and "we value you" are politeness. Answering the question the
   reader hasn't asked yet is care.

## Start in the reader's world

Before the first sentence, fix the context. Infostyle calls this "мир читателя": the reader's world,
which is not the author's.

- **Where are they coming from?** A push notification, a search result, a link from a colleague, a
  contract they're signing. That determines what they already know and what the first line must do.
- **What do they already believe?** Explaining a thing they know insults them; skipping a thing
  they don't loses them. Guess, then check against the user's brief.
- **What is their question?** Not the topic. The question. "Is my money safe" is a question;
  "custody model" is a topic.
- **What do they do after reading?** Click, sign, decide, explain to their boss. The text is done
  when they can do that.
- **What are they worried about?** See [Care](#care-name-the-worry-then-answer-it).

The same subject, written for a client who just saw a failed transfer and for a compliance officer
reviewing the transfer policy, is two texts. If your draft would work for both, it works for
neither.

## Facts over evaluation

An evaluation is a word that tells the reader what to think: *reliable, fast, convenient, unique,
professional, high-quality, modern, innovative, comfortable, best.* A fact is what they would need to
see to think it themselves.

Before: Наша надёжная платформа обеспечивает быстрые и удобные переводы.

After: Переводы внутри Швейцарии приходят за минуту. С 2023 года платформа не останавливалась ни
разу.

Before: Our experienced team delivers high-quality results.

After: The three people on this account have run custody operations for a combined 31 years. The
last audit had no findings.

The rule for the writer: **every evaluation is a debt.** Either pay it with the fact right after, or
cut it. "Reliable: zero downtime since 2023" is fine, if a little mechanical. "Zero downtime since
2023" alone is better; the reader supplies "reliable" and believes it more for having done so.

This does not mean the writer has no opinion. It means the opinion is stated once, plainly, as an
opinion ("We think the custody fee is the one to watch"), and the facts around it are facts.

## Benefit over feature

A feature is what the product has. A benefit is what the reader gets. Infostyle calls the failure
"свойства вместо пользы": listing properties and leaving the reader to work out why they'd care.

Before: 24/7 support. Multi-currency accounts. Biometric login.

After: If a transfer goes wrong at 2 a.m. on a Sunday, someone picks up. You hold CHF, EUR and USD
in one account, so a EUR invoice doesn't need a conversion first. Logging in is a fingerprint, not a
password you have to remember.

Test: after each feature, ask "so what?" out loud. If the answer isn't in the text, the reader is
doing your work.

Two guards, because this rule is easy to overdo:

- **Don't invent the benefit.** If you don't know what the reader gets from a feature, ask, or state
  the feature plainly. A fabricated benefit is a fabricated fact.
- **Don't preach the benefit.** "Imagine never worrying about passwords again" is marketing
  ventriloquism. State what happens: "You log in with a fingerprint."

## Stop-words: the categories, not the list

Infostyle's stop-words are words that carry no information for the reader. They fall into
categories, and the categories are more useful than any list, because the list differs by language
and the categories don't.

| Category | What it does | Russian | English |
|---|---|---|---|
| Intensifiers | Inflate without adding | очень, крайне, действительно, абсолютно, максимально | very, really, truly, extremely, incredibly |
| Evaluations | Tell the reader what to think | качественный, надёжный, уникальный, эффективный, удобный | high-quality, reliable, unique, effective, seamless |
| Clichés and stamps | Signal category, not subject | динамично развивающаяся, команда профессионалов, индивидуальный подход, широкий спектр | industry-leading, best-in-class, tailored solutions, a wide range of |
| Time parasites | Locate in a "now" that adds nothing | на сегодняшний день, в настоящее время, в наше время, в современном мире | in today's world, currently, at this point in time, nowadays |
| Bureaucratese | Hide the actor and the verb | осуществлять, являться, в рамках, в целях, данный, производится | facilitate, constitute, in the framework of, with a view to, said |
| Modal hedging | Weaken a plain statement | можете, возможно, в некоторых случаях, как правило | may, might, potentially, in some cases, generally |
| Generalisations | Claim everyone without evidence | все, многие, каждый, никто, известно, что | everyone, many, it is well known that, most people |
| Euphemisms | Soften a fact the reader needs | оптимизация численности, определённые трудности, нештатная ситуация | headcount optimisation, certain challenges, an incident |
| Introductory noise | Delay the content | стоит отметить, важно понимать, как известно, следует подчеркнуть | it is worth noting, it's important to understand, as we all know |
| Self-reference | Talk about the text instead of the subject | в данной статье мы рассмотрим, ниже приведены | in this article we will, the following outlines |

Handling rule, and this is where the skill departs from the mechanical version of infostyle:
**a stop-word is a flag, not a sentence.** When you hit one, ask what fact it was standing in for.
"Very fast" flags a missing number. "Certain challenges" flags a hidden problem. "Many clients"
flags a count or a name. Replace the flag with the fact when you have it; cut the flag when you
don't; and leave the word alone when it is doing real work (a "may" that marks genuine uncertainty,
a "really" in a quoted line of speech). Stripping every stop-word on sight produces the telegram.

## Syntax that reads itself

The infostyle syntax rules are about lowering the cost of each sentence.

- **One thought per sentence.** If the sentence has "and" joining two clauses with different
  subjects, or "which" introducing a second idea, split it. Then re-join with a real connector if
  the ideas depend on each other ("so," "because," "if," "поэтому," "если").
- **Subject, verb, close together, early.** The reader is holding the subject in memory until the
  verb arrives. Twelve words between them is a cost. Move the qualifier after the verb or into its
  own sentence.
- **Verbs, not nouns made from verbs.** "Проведение анализа" → "проанализировать." "The
  implementation of the review" → "we reviewed." Nominalisation is the single strongest marker of
  bureaucratic register in both languages.
- **Active voice, named actor.** "Решение было принято" → who decided. Passive stays only when the
  actor is unknown or truly irrelevant to the reader.
- **Participial constructions are read, not spoken.** In Russian, причастные and деепричастные
  обороты stack easily and read as a document. In English, the same job is done by -ing tails. Turn
  them into clauses with their own verb.
- **The important word goes where the sentence lands.** In Russian that is usually the end; in
  English, the end or the start, rarely the middle. "Переводы приходят за минуту" lands on the
  minute. "За минуту приходят переводы" lands on the transfers. Choose.
- **Homogeneous lists stay homogeneous.** If the sentence lists three things, they are the same
  part of speech and the same grammatical shape. Three items of three different shapes is a
  fragment of a list dressed as a sentence. Two items, or a real list.

None of this means short. A 30-word sentence with one thought, subject and verb up front, and a
real connector is easier than three 8-word fragments the reader has to reassemble.

## Structure for the diagonal reader

*Ясно, понятно* treats attention as a resource the reader spends reluctantly. Most readers scan first
and read second, if at all. Build for the scan.

- **The title is the text's answer, not its topic.** A reader who sees only the title should know
  the main point. "Переводы в ЕС теперь занимают один день, а не три" beats "Об изменениях в
  сроках переводов."
- **The first paragraph is the whole text in miniature.** Main point, main fact, what the reader
  should do. If they stop here, they leave with the essentials.
- **Each paragraph has one job, and its first sentence says what.** The scanner reads first
  sentences. Make them a spine that holds on its own.
- **Subheadings are the reader's questions where they'd ask them.** "What if I miss the cutoff?" at
  the place a reader would start to wonder.
- **Simple before complex, general before exception.** State the rule, then the exception. State
  the common case, then the edge case. Never open with the caveat.
- **Lists for things that are lists.** Steps, options, requirements. Not for three related sentences
  that were happier as a paragraph.
- **Cut the parts that serve the author.** History of the decision, thanks to the team, apologetic
  framing. If the reader must be able to find them, put them at the end or link them. This does not
  reach required wording or a real scope limit: risk warnings, regulatory formulas, defined terms,
  and what a document does not cover stay, because those serve the reader. Move them, never drop
  them.

Then reread as the scanner: title, first sentences, bold, list items. Does that skeleton tell the
truth? If a reader who only saw the skeleton would misunderstand, the skeleton is wrong, however
good the flesh.

## Explaining: known before unknown, example before rule

For any text that teaches something, infostyle's explanatory method:

- **Anchor in what the reader already knows.** "A dead-letter queue works like a returned-mail bin
  at the post office" borrows a model the reader has. Then correct the model where it breaks.
- **Example first, rule second.** People generalise from cases better than they instantiate rules.
  "On CHF 200,000, the fee is CHF 600 a year. That's 0.3%." Then the rule.
- **Anti-example beside the example.** What it is *not* is often the fastest way to fix the
  boundary: "This is a fee, not a penalty: you pay it whether or not you trade."
- **One picture where a picture would do.** A table for anything with two dimensions, a diagram for
  a flow. Prose describing a table is a cost.
- **Show the mechanism, not just the result.** "Transfers take a day because the receiving bank
  batches them overnight" lets the reader predict the next case. "Transfers take a day" does not.
- **The detail that makes it real.** One specific, concrete, checkable detail per explanation. The
  time, the amount, the name of the screen. Abstractions don't teach.

## Care: name the worry, then answer it

The part of *Ясно, понятно* that most texts skip. A reader arriving at a text about a failed
transfer is not curious about transfers. They are worried about their money. Text that ignores the
worry and delivers the information anyway is correct and useless.

- **Say the worry out loud, in their words, once.** "If a transfer fails, the first question is
  whether the money left your account." Not "We understand this may be concerning."
- **Answer it immediately.** "It didn't. A failed transfer is reversed within the hour and shows in
  your history as 'returned.'"
- **Then the information.** Now they can read it.

Care also means not deciding for the reader. Infostyle warns against "решать за читателя": telling
them what they want, how they feel, what they'll love. "You'll love how easy this is" decides for
them. "This takes three fields" lets them decide.

And it means the inconvenient fact goes in. If there's a cutoff, a fee, a delay, a limit, it is in
the text, near the place the reader would trip on it, stated as plainly as the good news. A reader
who finds the catch in the fine print doesn't come back.

## Where infostyle and this skill part ways

Infostyle is a school with a house style, and some of the house style conflicts with the measured
evidence in `evidence.md`. When they conflict, the evidence wins.

| Infostyle reflex | This skill |
|---|---|
| Cut every stop-word | Treat it as a flag for a missing fact; cut only when there is none |
| Shorter is better | Easier is better; short fragments in a row read as machine text |
| No opinions, only facts | One opinion, stated as an opinion, is what makes the text a person's |
| Strip adverbs and modals | Keep the ones that change meaning; real uncertainty stays marked |
| Every sentence a fact | Keep the connective tissue; a sentence can join two facts |
| Uniform, "clean" tone | Uneven rhythm and a plain negative where warranted |

The reason to hold the line: a text that has been through the infostyle grinder is grammatically
correct, factually dense, and reads like every other text that went through the grinder. It has
lost the writer. The skill's job is to keep the reader-first commitments (facts, truth, care,
structure) and let the writer's rhythm survive them. "Пиши, сокращай" told a generation to cut. The
harder skill is knowing what to leave.

## Exit check

In addition to the SKILL.md exit check:

1. Can you say in one line who the reader is, what they already know, and what they'll do after?
2. Any evaluation (reliable, fast, unique, качественный, удобный) not followed by its fact?
3. Any feature without a "so what" answered in the text?
4. Each stop-word you hit: did you replace it with the fact, or just delete it? Any deleted where
   the fact was available?
5. Any nominalised verb, hidden actor, or participial stack?
6. Does the skeleton (title, first sentences, bold, list items) tell the truth alone?
7. Is the reader's likely worry named and answered before the information?
8. Is every inconvenient fact (fee, cutoff, limit, delay) in the text, near where it bites?
9. Any "you'll love," "imagine," or other deciding-for-the-reader?
10. Did the cutting go too far? Read it aloud. If it sounds like a telegram, put the connectors
    back.
